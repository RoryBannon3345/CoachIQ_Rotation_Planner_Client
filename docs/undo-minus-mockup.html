Undo and minus — ten ways to take a tap back

Today the bottom bar has two controls that both "take something back", and nothing on screen says which to reach for. Every frame below is the same moment: set 1, we serve at 14–11, rotation 3, Hailey serving. The coach has just noticed that Grace's Serve In reads 2 but should be 1: a stray tap a few rallies ago. The last tap (Hailey, Serve In, which scored us a point) was right, so Undo alone can't fix it without also taking Hailey's tap away.

What the two buttons do today. ↶ Undo reverses the last thing recorded in this game, in time order: a count tap and the rally letters it inferred, an Us/Them point, or a re-align. Up to 400 steps, and it works across every set of the game, so it can change a set you aren't looking at. − arms a one-shot mode: the next count you tap goes down by one, wherever it is, and the mode switches itself off. It leaves the rally log alone, except that a minus on the cell that opened the current rally cancels that rally. So the real split is "my last tap was wrong" (Undo) versus "that cell is wrong" (minus). The options below either fold the second job into the cell itself, fold both jobs into one button, or keep both and make the roles impossible to confuse.
Recommendation: A, hold a count to take one away. Runner-up: E, Undo opens a list.
  • A leaves one button in the bar with one meaning. Undo means "my last tap was wrong". Everything else is "that cell is wrong", fixed on the cell, which is where the coach is already looking.
  • A hold can't fire by accident while tapping fast, unlike the split cell in B, and there is no mode to forget you are in, unlike F, I and today's −.
  • The open-rally cancel rule carries over untouched: a hold on the cell that opened the rally is the same minus tap the app already handles.
  • Its one cost is that a hold is invisible until you know it. A one-time toast the first time the record screen opens, plus a line in the ⋯ menu, covers that.
  • E is the strongest answer if you would rather have nothing hidden in a gesture: one button, and the coach sees exactly what each row will change before it changes. It also fixes the quiet surprise that Undo today can reach into another set.
Why not the others: B puts a subtract target under a fast-tapping thumb. C is a gesture too, and a bigger one, on a row that also has to scroll. D and J make every fix three taps through a sheet. F hides the minus inside the Undo button, so it is still two jobs on one control, just less visible. G makes Undo disappear after five seconds. H and I are the smallest changes but keep two buttons, and I adds a mode banner to a screen that is already full.

Fold the subtract into the cell — the bar keeps only Undo and Export

A · Hold a count to take one away Recommendedthe − button goes; press and hold any cell

Tap adds one, as today. Hold a count for about half a second and it gets a red ring with a "−1" tag; let go and it drops by one. Move your finger and nothing happens. The first time the record screen opens, a toast explains the hold. Needs -webkit-touch-callout:none and user-select:none on the cells so iPhone doesn't show its own menu. A hold on the cell that opened the current rally cancels the rally, exactly as − does now.

B · A slim "−" strip on every cell the subtract target is always visible

Each count becomes two targets: the big top part adds, the thin band along the bottom takes one away. No gesture, no mode, nothing to learn. Trade-off: a thumb moving fast can hit the strip instead of the top, and when the 12-row density guard shrinks the rows there is no room for the strip, so it would hide and the coach would need another way on a full roster.

C · Swipe a row to reveal four "−" buttons mail style

Drag a player's row to the left and it slides over to show a red "−" under each of the four columns. Tap one to take one away; tap anywhere else to close. Keeps the cells single-purpose. Trade-off: a horizontal swipe has to be told apart from a vertical scroll on a list that scrolls, and the gesture is no easier to discover than a hold.

Fold the subtract into a sheet or a list — one button, nothing hidden in a gesture

D · Tap a player's name to fix their counts a sheet with four steppers

Each name gets a small chevron. Tapping it opens a sheet for that player in this set with four steppers; − and + adjust, Done closes. Every fix is deliberate and visible. Trade-off: a one-tap correction becomes three taps (name, −, Done), and the sheet covers the score and the serving row while it is open.

E · Undo opens a list of recent taps Runner-upone button, both jobs

The bar reads "↶ Undo ▾". Tapping it shows the last five things recorded, newest first. The top row is a true Undo: it reverses that entry and the rally letters it inferred, exactly as the button does today. Any lower count row takes one away from that cell, which is what − does today, so the letters already in the log stay; lower points and re-aligns are shown for context only. Entries from another set say which set, so Undo stops being a surprise. Trade-off: an ordinary undo is now two taps, and the list is a new piece of UI to maintain.

F · One button: tap to undo, hold to arm minus the literal merge

A tap on "↶ Undo" undoes the last entry. Hold it and it turns red, "− Tap a count"; the next count you tap goes down by one and the button returns to normal. The − button is gone and the bar has two cells. Trade-off: this is today's minus mode with a hidden way in. It still has two jobs on one control, and the coach still has to decide which job before pressing.

Keep both jobs, make the roles impossible to confuse

G · Undo moves into the toast; the bar keeps one Fix button Gmail style

After every tap a toast above the bar says what was recorded, with an Undo link, and stays for five seconds. The bar has only "− Fix" and Export, so there is one button and it means "that cell is wrong". Older undos live in the ⋯ menu as "Undo last". Trade-off: the easiest undo is the one you have to catch within five seconds; after that it is a menu trip.

H · Say what each button does labels only, no behaviour change

Three equal columns: "↶ Undo last", "− Fix a count", "Export". The words carry the distinction that the icons don't. Nothing else changes, so this is the smallest possible change. Trade-off: still two buttons for two jobs, and the coach still has to answer "was it my last tap?" before choosing.

I · Fix mode takes over the bar the mode can't be mistaken for the normal screen

Tapping − swaps the whole bottom bar for a red banner, "Tap a count to take one away", with a Cancel button, and every count shows a small − badge. The red outlines today are easy to miss; this isn't. Undo is unchanged. Trade-off: still two buttons, and a mode is still a mode: the coach has to get in and out of it.

J · A whole-set edit sheet − becomes ✎ Fix

"✎ Fix" opens a sheet with every ticked player and four steppers each, so several corrections can be made in one visit, then Done. Minus mode and its red outlines go away; Undo stays for the last tap. Trade-off: three taps for one fix, the steppers are small on a full roster, and the sheet hides the live score while it is open.
